A college that hires itself.

You say what you want to learn. AULA hires a faculty of agents, designs your semester, and runs the whole institution — attendance, lessons, projects, exams. Teachers are graded on whether you actually learn. When you struggle, they get penalized and rewrite how they teach.

Faculty reinforcement loop

The student never loses points. The teacher does.

Outcomes are reward signal. Reward flows to faculty grades; failure triggers a methodology rewrite from the Provost. This is the mechanic that makes AULA more than a chatbot tutor.

− reward Penalties (teacher only)

You can't answer a probe questionSmall negative reward to the professor. You're untouched — it's a signal the explanation didn't land.
You fail a mid-termFull negative reward. You don't pass; you re-enroll the unit. The teacher's grade drops hard.
Attendance / momentum stallsMinor penalty + TA nudge. Persistent stall escalates to the Provost.

+ reward Rewards

You answer correctly & passPositive reward to the teaching agent; their grade rises and their method is reinforced.
You finish the semesterFaculty graded on your final mastery — the only score that ultimately matters.
On failure →
1
Examiner gatesMarks the unit failed, blocks progression, flags the responsible teacher.
2
Teacher grade dropsFull negative reward applied to the professor's running grade.
3
Provost rewrites methodShips an updated teaching plan — new pacing, new examples, new modality.
4
Student re-enrollsSame unit, new approach. No penalty to you — just another shot.
loop continues until mastery ✓

Two kanbans, mirrored

Every student has a board (lessons → doing → submitted → graded). Every teacher has one too (prep → teaching → assessing → reviewing). They sync at the module boundary so handoffs are explicit.

Attendance = heartbeats

Paperclip's agent heartbeats become class attendance. Sessions are scheduled; the Registrar logs presence; momentum feeds the reward loop.

Results = the transcript

Exams, project grades and the running teacher grades all land in one record — your transcript and the faculty's performance review, side by side.

Before I build the screens — three calls for you

Decisions that change the design.

Q1 · Tone of penalty

Should faculty penalties feel visible & dramatic (a teacher gets "fired" / replaced) or quiet & systemic (just a grade nudge)? It sets the emotional register.

Q2 · Live vs instant

Should hiring & build play out live (watch it assemble, Paperclip-style) or resolve instantly to a finished plan you review?

Q3 · Anime depth

How far does the anime theme go — just avatars, or full character UI (named personas, dialogue bubbles, voice)? I'll wire it as a theme either way.
